## Rev. 1.5 corrections to the Rev. 1.1 facts above

Checked line by line for the items used in this study. Tables 3.1, 3.2, 3.6, 3.7 (VIH), 4.2, 4.3, 4.4, 5.2, 7.1 and 7.2 read the same in Rev. 1.5 as in Rev. 1.1 (grep of the Rev. 1.5 text for the same rows). Two differences matter:

1. **A VBUS divider is required in the bus-powered design too.** Rev. 1.5 sec. 2.3: "There are two relevant restrictions on the VBUS pin voltage in self-powered and bus-powered configurations ... A resistor divider (or functionally-equivalent circuit) on VBUS is required to meet these specifications and ensure reliable device operation." Figure 2.5 (bus-powered) now shows 22.1 kΩ (series) and 47.5 kΩ (to ground) on the VBUS sense pin, with VREGIN tied directly to connector VBUS. Revision history, Rev. 1.2 (November 2017): "added the resistor divider to Figure 2.5 Bus-Powered Connection Diagram". Rev. 1.1's Figure 2.5 (no divider) is superseded. Divider output at 5.00 V VBUS: 5.00 × 47.5 / (22.1 + 47.5) = 3.41 V (arithmetic), which meets VIH = VIO − 0.6 = 2.7 V at VIO = 3.3 V and stays below the 5.8 V / VIO + 2.5 V limit.
2. **RSTb is driven by the chip.** Rev. 1.5 sec. 2.1 and Table 3.3 Note 1: "The RSTb pin will be driven low during power-on and power failure reset events." It must not be tied hard to a supply rail; the 1 kΩ pull-up to VIO is the recommended form.

Also from the Rev. 1.5 revision history: "RTS control signaling and RS485 features are not supported below 1200 baud" (Rev. 1.2); not relevant to uploads at 115200 baud.

## 2. Loads on 3V3_FTDI

### Okapi (U23, `USB_ISP_Feather.kicad_sch`)

Net `/USB_ISP_Feather/3V3_FTDI` has **4 nodes**: U23 pin 10 3V3OUT, U23 pin 11 RESET#, U23 pin 20 VCCIO, and C25 0.1 µF. Nothing else. The external load is the I/O cells only: TXD into the MCU through R38 330 Ω, and the two LED cathode pins, which sink current (D4's anode is on `3V3_CORE`, 45 nodes, which the logger's own supply feeds, not the bridge). **On Okapi the bridge regulator carries only the bridge.** CP2102N: 9.5 mA typ at 115200 baud plus 0.2 mA pull-up (Table 3.2) against 100 mA total (Table 3.6). Fits, with about 90 mA spare.

### Margay (U2, `Digital_Core.kicad_sch` and `Power_Sensors.kicad_sch`)

Net `3V3_FTDI` has **6 nodes**: U2 pins 10, 11, 20, C9 0.1 µF, **Q2 FDN360P drain**, and **U10 TLV3691 IN+**.

- **Q2 ties 3V3_FTDI to the logger's 3V3 rail when USB is present.** Q2 (P-channel) has source on `3V3`, drain on `3V3_FTDI`, gate on `Net-(Q2-G)`, which R19 10 kΩ pulls to 3V3 and Q3 (2N7002W, gate on VUSB, R21 10 kΩ to GND) pulls to GND when VUSB is present. With USB plugged in, Q2 is on and the bridge regulator feeds the whole `3V3` rail alongside U6 TPS79733 (VIN on `VBAT+`, 50 mA continuous, TPS797 SLVS332J "IO Output current 0 ... 50 mA"). With no battery on the bench, the bridge regulator is the only supply for that rail.
- **U10 decides the power source.** TLV3691 compares 3V3_FTDI (IN+) with VBAT_SW/2 (R17/R18 10 kΩ/10 kΩ, IN−); its output drives U9 TPS2104 EN. TPS2104 function table (SLVS235A p. 2): EN high selects IN2 (here VUSB), EN low selects IN1 (here VBAT_SW). U9's output `VMAIN` feeds U7 TPS7A05 for `3V3_AUX` (RTC VCC, SD card and the rest, 20 nodes), which is therefore not on 3V3_FTDI.

Acceptable; dropping D1 is an option to weigh in the redesign.

## Blocking problems found while tracing (A, B)

**A. Margay's USB-present comparator is fed by the bridge rail.** This is fine as long as 3V3_FTDI is 0 V whenever USB is absent. Problem B is a way that it might not be.

**B. The CP2102N's I/O pins are not tolerant of voltage when the chip is unpowered; the FT231X's are.**

- FT231X, FT_000565 v1.4 Table 5.1: "DC Input Voltage – High Impedance Bi-directional (powered from VCCIO) −0.3 to +5.8 V", and page 1 "Tolerant of 5V input" (revision 1.2: "Clarified IO is tolerant of 5V input"). The limit is absolute, not referenced to VCCIO.
- CP2102N, Rev. 1.5 Table 3.10: "Voltage on UART pins, GPIO, VBUS, RSTb, or any other non-power, non-USB pin: VIO > 3.3 V: 5.8 V; **VIO < 3.3 V: VIO + 2.5 V**". With the bridge unpowered (USB unplugged, the normal field state), VIO = 0 V and the limit is **2.5 V**.
- On both boards the MCU's TXD0 drives the bridge's RXD through 330 Ω (Margay R9, Okapi R40; Okapi's TX net also reaches the Boron J9 and the Feather MS1). A UART idles high, so in the field the bridge's RXD sits at **3.3 V, 0.8 V over the CP2102N's absolute maximum**, around the clock, for the whole winter.
- What happens then is UNVERIFIED without hardware. The datasheet itself accepts a current-limited violation in one place (Rev. 1.5 sec. 2.3, the VBUS divider: "the current limitation of the resistor divider prevents high VBUS pin leakage current, even though the VIO + 2.5 V specification is not strictly met while the device is not powered"), but that divider is 22.1 kΩ, not 330 Ω. If the pin back-feeds VIO, then on Margay 3V3_FTDI rises above 0 V, U10 can see 3V3_FTDI > VBAT_SW/2 (and VBAT_SW is 0 V whenever U8 is switched off), switch U9 to VUSB, which is absent, and drop `3V3_AUX` (RTC VCC, SD card). On both boards it would be a battery drain through the bridge. Neither has been measured.
- The LED pins are less exposed: Okapi's D4 and Margay's D2 anodes sit on the logger's 3.3 V rail, and the cathode pins see 3.3 V less the LED's forward voltage at leakage current (not computed; red VF is 1.9 V at 8 mA), likely under 2.5 V. UNVERIFIED.

**Remedy (a proposal for Andy).** Put a buffer with partial-power-down protection (Ioff) in the MCU TXD → bridge RXD line, powered from the bridge's VDD: when the bridge is off, the buffer's output is 0 V and its input tolerates the MCU's 3.3 V. Candidates are single-gate 74LVC1G or 74AUP1G buffers (TI SN74LVC1G125/SN74LVC1G34 class). Part choice, its datasheet and its Ioff figure were not fetched here: UNVERIFIED. A cheaper partial remedy is a larger series resistor (10 kΩ gives an RC of about 70 ns with the 7 pF pin capacitance of Table 3.7, harmless at 57600 baud), which limits the current but does not bring the pin within its rating. The bridge's TXD → MCU RXD direction needs nothing: an unpowered output only presents 0 V or high impedance to the MCU, as the FT231X does now.

## Verdict

**The CP2102N-A02-GQFN24 works as a replacement by redesign, not as a drop-in.** Every function the boards use maps onto it: TXD, RXD, an active-low DTR that resets the ATmega1284P through the same 100 nF exactly as avrdude 6.3 drives it, TX/RX LED outputs, a 3.3 V regulator with twice the FT231X's headroom (100 mA total against 50 mA), drivers built into Linux and, by third-party account, macOS 11+, and Silicon Labs drivers for 10C4:EA60 on Windows Update for Windows 10 and 11. The package fits each board's present site (no courtyard conflict on Margay, one on Okapi), but the pinout differs completely and the local fan-out must be rerouted.

**Blocking problem, both boards (B):** the CP2102N's I/O pins are rated to VIO + 2.5 V when VIO < 3.3 V, so 2.5 V with the bridge unpowered, while the MCU's idle UART TX holds the bridge's RXD at 3.3 V through 330 Ω for the whole field season. The FT231X is rated to 5.8 V there regardless of VCCIO. This must be designed out (proposal: a partial-power-down buffer such as SN74LVC1G125, powered from the bridge's VDD) before the swap is sound. The consequence of leaving it is UNVERIFIED without hardware: at least an absolute-maximum violation, and possibly back-powering and battery drain.

**Blocking on the present Margay v3.0 topology only (A):** if B back-feeds 3V3_FTDI, U10 can switch U9 to an absent VUSB and drop 3V3_AUX in the field. The v3.1.0 power-path decision removes U10 and U9, which removes A.

**Not blocking, but a production step:** the TX/RX LEDs are dark until the configuration ROM is written (Silicon Labs `cp210xsmt` from an Xpress Configurator file, Linux binary UNVERIFIED; or open-source `cp210x-cfg -L 1`, byte layout cross-checked against the Linux kernel here).

**Open, to settle with one board or one chip:** Windows Update automatic versus optional delivery for PID 0xEA60 on a clean Windows 11 machine; `cp210x-cfg -L 1` on an A02 part; macOS built-in driver against an Apple source; the 47.5 kΩ and 4.7 µF stock at Digi-Key.

Changes per board:

- **Margay:** new symbol and footprint for U2 with the section 1 pin map; delete R3, R4, C7, C8 and join D+/D−; add the 22.1 kΩ / 47.5 kΩ VBUS divider from VUSB; RSTb 1 kΩ to 3V3_FTDI; 4.7 µF + 0.1 µF on VREGIN (the node behind D1 has no capacitor today), on VDD (C9 plus a 4.7 µF) and on VIO; TX_LED/RX_LED to pins 14/13; Ioff buffer in the MCU TX → RXD line; reroute the bridge's fan-out; README lines 233 and 251; a configuration step in provisioning. Do it together with the v3.1.0 power-path work, which already takes the bridge regulator off the core.
- **Okapi:** new symbol and footprint for U23; delete R34, R35, C23, C24 and join D+/D−; add the VBUS divider from `/USB_ISP_Feather/VUSB`; RSTb 1 kΩ to 3V3_FTDI; 4.7 µF + 0.1 µF on VDD/VIO (C25 kept; C21, C22 serve VREGIN); LED cathode resistors R39/R36 to pins 14/13; Ioff buffer in the TX → RXD line (R40); move C25 clear of the larger courtyard; reroute the fan-out; README line 167; the same configuration step.

# USB-to-UART bridge: second source or replacement for the FTDI FT231X

Status: COMPLETE (2026-11-11)

Agent report, 2026-11-11. Question (Andy): "I don't know of another good USB bridge option other than the FTDI chip. I know that Arduino uses a custom-bootloaded MCU, but I don't know if at QTY 100 I'm really at that point."

## What this report establishes

1. **The FT231X is out of stock at Digi-Key today, in both packages.** FT231XQ-R (QFN-20, the part on both boards): "In-Stock: 0", "5,000 expected in stock on 18-Jan-2027", lead time "50 Weeks". FT231XS-R (SSOP-20): 0, "2,000 expected in stock on 16-Mar-2027", 50 weeks. This is a present supply problem, separate from the forced-sale order. Broker stock exists (findchips: Win Source 11,790, Chip Stock 24,070 and others), but brokers are not authorized and carry counterfeit risk. Newark shows 108.
2. **No bridge is pin- or footprint-compatible with the FT231X.** Every candidate needs a new footprint and new routing (table below).
3. **A credible fixed-function second source exists: Silicon Labs CP2102N.** It is rated −40 to +85 °C, needs no crystal, has a 3.3 V regulator output (100 mA total), and is in stock at Digi-Key. Pick the QFN24 if you want a real DTR pin: **the QFN20 has RTS and CTS but no DTR.** RTS works for auto-reset, because avrdude drives DTR and RTS together (checked in the avrdude source).
4. **Silicon Labs is itself changing owner.** Texas Instruments agreed on 4 Feb 2026 to buy it (about $7.5 billion, close expected in the first half of 2027). TI plans to move production into its own fabs, including US 300 mm fabs. On Andy's criterion 1 that is probably a gain over the long term. The transition will bring change notices.
5. **Infineon CY7C65213A is a second credible candidate.** It is −40 to +85 °C (the -XI suffix), needs no crystal, has the full modem set including DTR, and supports the inbox CDC class driver. Digi-Key has 864 of the 32-QFN at $2.0964 at 100.
6. **Rejected on function or sourcing.** (1) Microchip MCP2221A: "UART UTx and URx Pins Only", so it has no DTR or RTS and cannot auto-reset. (2) Microchip MCP2200: needs a 12 MHz crystal, and its RTS is hardware flow control driven by the receive buffer, which cannot pulse reset. (3) WCH CH340, CH343 and CH9102: Digi-Key does not stock the bare chips. The crystal-free CH340C/N/K/E/B are rated −20 to 70 °C, except batches whose number begins with 4. The CH340's Windows Update driver 3.8 broke avrdude uploads in 2023. (4) Prolific PL2303: Digi-Key returns no results, and Prolific's Windows driver locks out older revisions and counterfeits.
7. **The custom-MCU route costs little per unit at 100 boards.** Its cost is a second programming step and owning a firmware. The VID can be free through pid.codes, because both boards are open hardware (CC BY-SA 4.0) with GPL firmware, though eligibility is pid.codes' decision. Windows 10 and 11 load the inbox `usbser.sys` for any CDC-ACM device with no INF and no signing.

**Footprint compatibility.** None. The FT231XQ is a 4x4 mm QFN-20 and the FT231XS a 3.9 mm SSOP-20. The CP2102N QFN-20 is 3x3 mm with a different pinout. The CP2102N QFN-24 is pin-compatible with the CP2104, and the QFN-28 with the CP2101/2/9, not with FTDI. The MCP2200 and CH340T/R SSOP-20 have 5.3 mm bodies and different pinouts. The CH9102F follows the CP2104 QFN-24 pinout "albeit with some small differences" (CNX).

**What a replacement must also carry on these boards (from the schematics).** Both boards wire TX, RX and DTR. Margay drives TX_LED and RX_LED from the FT231X, and Okapi has ALIVE_LED on the USB sheet. A `3V3_FTDI` net leaves Margay's USB sheet for `Power_Sensors.kicad_sch`; its role was not traced. The CP2102N regulator gives 3.3 V at up to 100 mA, including its own current, and its QFN24 and QFN28 have TXT and RXT LED functions on GPIO.0 and GPIO.1.

## Part 2: the custom-MCU bridge at 100 boards

**(a) USB VID and PID.**

- USB-IF: a vendor ID without a logo license is US$6,000, one time. Membership is US$5,000 a year and includes one. Without a logo license "you are not authorized to use the USB logo".
- pid.codes: free PIDs under VID 0x1209 for open-source hardware. It requires a public repository with "modifiable PCB design files" under "a recognized open source or open source hardware license", and both hardware and firmware must be open if both exist. Margay and Okapi hardware are CC BY-SA 4.0 and their software GPL-3.0, so they appear to qualify. Acceptance is pid.codes' decision.
- Microchip: a 2010 form offers a free PID under VID 0x04D8, "solely with Microchip microcontrollers", "revocable and non-transferable". Its current status is UNVERIFIED (Microchip's page returned 403).
- LUFA test PIDs (0x03EB:0x2040, CDC demo 0x2044): "should not be used in commercial designs under any circumstances", and "No devices using this VID/PID combination may be released to the general public". Shipping on them invites collisions with other LUFA devices and breaks the terms Atmel donated them under.
- Arduino's 0x2341 belongs to Arduino. No public grant for derivative boards was found, so treat it as unavailable.

The VID does not decide the Windows driver: a CDC-ACM device loads `usbser.sys` by class match whatever its VID. The VID decides uniqueness, and with it the Arduino IDE's board auto-detection.

**(b) The extra production step.**

- ATmega16U2: it ships on the internal 8 MHz RC with CKDIV8. Its USB needs an external crystal ("must be provided from an external crystal or an external clock input"). Each board needs fuses and firmware written over ISP before USB works. Arduino's factory image combines a DFU bootloader with USB-serial, for later reflashing with `dfu-programmer` (Arduino docs, via summary). Whether a blank 16U2 from Microchip already holds a DFU bootloader is UNVERIFIED.
- A small shop does this with the same AVR ISP programmer and a second 6-pin header or pogo pads. That is a second pass per board beside the 1284P bootloader, and it fits as a step in `NW-Provision`.
- SAM D11: programmed over SWD, a different tool (a CMSIS-DAP probe) and a different toolchain. Arduino does exactly this on the Nano Every, where the ATSAMD11D14A runs Arduino's open MuxTO firmware as the USB-serial bridge and UPDI programmer.

**(c) Windows driver signing.** Confirmed: on Windows 10 and later, "If your device belongs to the communications and CDC control device class, Usbser.sys loads automatically. You don't need to write your own INF file" (Microsoft Learn, 2025-06-11). Class 02, subclass 02 is the trigger. No signing is needed. Windows 7 and 8 would still need an INF, not checked. macOS lists AppleUSBCDCACMData (third party), and Linux has `cdc_acm` (from memory).

**(d) Firmware maintenance.** You would own a USB stack and the DTR-to-reset, baud-rate and LED logic. A bug in it reaches every board, and the firmware becomes a versioned artifact beside the main firmware. Arduino's 16U2 USB-serial firmware is LUFA-based. The burden is modest, but it is permanent and it is the main cost of this route. That assessment is my reading, not a measured figure.

**(e) Parts cost and area at 100.** FT231XQ-R: $2.20, QFN-20 4x4 (16 mm²), no crystal. ATmega16U2-MU: $2.70, 32-VQFN 5x5 (25 mm²), plus a crystal and two capacitors (crystal price not fetched) plus ISP pads, 15,777 in stock. ATSAMD11C14A-SSUT: $1.53 at the 25 tier, 14-SOIC, 8,078 in stock. It is crystal-less by USB clock recovery (third-party datasheet summary, UNVERIFIED) and runs on 1.62 to 3.63 V only, which suits 3.3 V boards. RP2040: Raspberry Pi gives −40 to +85 °C (Mouser and the Pico brief say −20 °C, a conflict). It needs external flash and a crystal (from memory), which makes it more part than the job needs.

**Answer to "at QTY 100, am I at that point?"** On cost per unit, yes: the VID can be free, the driver is inbox, and the SAM D11 costs less than the FT231X. On effort, the route costs a second programming pass per board and a firmware to own for the life of the product. Its resilience gain is real but specific. The ATmega1284P already makes both boards dependent on Microchip, which owns US fabs and Thai and Philippine back-end sites. A Microchip bridge therefore removes a maker from the BoM without adding a dependency, and the firmware is yours whoever owns any chip maker.

## Part 3: recommendation (PROPOSAL, Andy's decision)

**Proposal: approve the Silicon Labs CP2102N-A02-GQFN24 as the second source now. Keep the FT231X as the incumbent. Hold the custom route (SAM D11 or ATmega16U2) as the documented third path.**

Reasons, in Andy's order:

1. **Geopolitical resilience.** CP2102N: US-owned and passing to TI, with wafers from TSMC and SMIC and assembly and test in Taiwan and South Korea (company-wide statement; per-part site not published). FT231X: owner under a forced-sale order, assembly sites not published, and zero Digi-Key stock until January 2027. Whether the two are "assembled in different countries" cannot be confirmed, because FTDI does not publish its sites. Ask FTDI and Silicon Labs for the assembly site of these specific parts. The custom route on Microchip scores best here, with owned back-end sites, 67 % in-house assembly in fiscal 2025, and US fabs.
2. **Wide-temperature quality.** All three paths are rated −40 to +85 °C with no margin at −40 °C. The WCH crystal-free CH340 variants fail this unless the batch is checked.
3. **Batch consistency.** Buy only from authorized stock: Digi-Key stocks the CP2102N, FT231X (when restocked), CY7C65213A, ATmega16U2 and SAM D11, but not WCH or Prolific. A custom bridge's behavior is fixed by your own firmware.
4. **Workers' rights.** Microchip and Infineon have KnowTheChain 2025 scores in the semiconductors report (Microchip RBA Full, 11/100; Infineon RBA Regular, 8/100). Silicon Labs and FTDI were not benchmarked or not checked. Evidence is thin for every candidate.

The Infineon CY7C65213A-32LTXI is a reasonable alternative second source if Andy prefers a European maker. It is cheaper at 100 ($2.0964), but its assembly sites were not checked and its default enumeration mode is UNVERIFIED.

**Open items, in order:**

1. Trace what `3V3_FTDI` powers on Margay, and check that the replacement's regulator can carry it.
2. Confirm the CP2102N default PID's Windows driver path on a clean Windows 11 machine.
3. Request assembly-site statements from FTDI and Silicon Labs.
4. Fetch the FT231X datasheet itself; the ftdichip.com download was blocked this session.
5. If the custom route is pursued, apply to pid.codes, and bench-test SAM D11 crystal-less USB at −40 °C.
